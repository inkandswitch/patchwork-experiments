# Students are most of Aachen's growth

Between 2000 and 2020 Aachen gained about 20,000 residents, and almost nine in ten of them were students. Student numbers rose from 34,000 to 60,000 while everyone else grew by 1%. Since 2021 it has been the other way round: fewer students, and about 7,000 more other residents. This page shows where students live and how many flats they need.

Student numbers are an input, taken from the enrolment record. The model then decides where the students live.

## Where students live

In 2025 the model splits Aachen's 56,000 students like this:

- **19,600 live outside the city**, in nearby towns, across the border, or still at an address elsewhere. The 2022 census counted about one in three enrolled students as not living in Aachen.
- **4,000 live with their parents.** The census found 11% of resident students did.
- **5,300 live in student halls**, about as many places as the Studierendenwerk has.
- **27,100 rent on the open market**, on average 1.8 to a flat, so they need about 15,100 flats.

```chart
title: Students enrolled, living in Aachen, and renting on the market
scenarios: real

```

The number of hall places hardly changed, so all the growth landed on the open market. The share living outside rises from 25% to 35% as rents climb. The 25% in 2000 is a guess; the 35% matches the census.

## A student needs half a flat, but students grew fastest

100 students need about 27 flats; 100 other residents need about 53. Still, from 2000 to 2020 students added about 6,700 flats of demand, and everyone else about 6,000. Four fifths of the 6,000 came from smaller households, not more people: the average household shrank from 2.0 to 1.9 people, so the same people needed more flats.

```chart
title: Non-student population and household size
scenarios: real
series: population, householdSize
years: 2000–2025
```

From 2020 to 2025 it reversed. Students needed about 1,300 fewer flats, while other households needed 6,500 more.

The non-student population is the registered population minus the two thirds of students who are registered here. Before 2011 it is joined to the state's figures, and the jumps from the 2011 census and the 2003 second-home tax are smoothed out.

# What comes next

The forecasts expect a dip in new students in 2026, then more students than ever, about 66,000 by 2040. Meanwhile the rest of the population stops growing. In the model, rents peak around 2027 and then ease slightly. By 2040 students are again the main driver: three fifths of the rise since 2000.

## The forecasts

- **First-year students** follow the education ministers' forecast (KMK) to 2035. NRW has almost no school leavers in 2026, because its schools are going back to 13 years. The low variant follows NRW, where first-years fall by 18% that year; the high variant follows Germany as a whole, down 3%; medium sits in between. All three reach about 10,500 first-years in Aachen by 2035, and stay there.
- **Other residents** follow the city's own forecast to 2039 in the medium and high variants, and the state's in the low variant. The city expects slow growth, but almost all of it is students, so the number of other residents stays level. The state expects Aachen to shrink.
- **Household size** follows the state's household forecast, which barely changes after 2025.

Rents ease after 2027 because building keeps adding flats while demand from other households levels off. The variants differ mainly in how many other residents there will be, not in students.

Treat these as directions, not predictions. The KMK student forecast was built on an older school-leaver forecast; the newer one (September 2026) expects 13,000 to 16,000 fewer school leavers a year in NRW from 2027, so first-year numbers after 2027 are probably 10–15% too high. The model has no interest rates, no inflation and no nationwide boom. It also probably overstates what each extra student does to rents (see the natural-experiment notebook).
